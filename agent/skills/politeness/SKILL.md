## When to Use
Use when the user asks to make text polite, concise, professional, less AI-sounding, or to fix grammar/rephrase while preserving meaning.

## Procedure
1. Remove filler openers such as “In today's fast-paced world” and “It's important to note that.”
2. Remove hedging boilerplate such as “While X, it's worth considering Y” unless the contrast changes the meaning.
3. Avoid forced rule-of-three phrasing such as “robust, scalable, and efficient.” Keep only concrete, needed descriptors.
4. Remove empty summaries such as “In conclusion” when they only restate prior points.
5. Avoid overused AI words including delve, leverage, seamless, tapestry, testament, underscore, and elevate.
6. Remove fake balance such as “On one hand... on the other hand” when there is no clear stance.
7. Remove vague praise such as “great question” and “a powerful tool.”
8. Avoid em-dash plus parallelism overload, uniform paragraph lengths, and vague wording with no concrete detail.
9. Avoid confident claims without sources. Soften or attribute them when evidence is missing.
10. Remove repetition, preserve the original meaning, fix grammar, keep it concise, and stay polite.

## Pitfalls
- Do not change the user's intended meaning to sound smoother.
- Do not add new claims, examples, or praise unless the user asks.
- Do not make the text colder while making it concise; keep a courteous tone.
- Do not use the banned words as replacements.

## Verification
1. The revised text preserves the original meaning.
2. The revised text is shorter or no longer than needed.
3. No listed AI-slop patterns remain.
4. The tone is polite and grammatically correct.
